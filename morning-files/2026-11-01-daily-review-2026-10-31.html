An exclusive conversation with the developer of REA, the viral AI reverse engineering tool

Scientific American — October 30, 2026

  • REA (Reverse Engineer Anything), by pseudonymous developer Morluto, lets AI agents pull apart compiled software and reconstitute it; its community has used it on printers, televisions and cars, and the project has more than 400,000 GitHub stars.
  • REA provides the pipelines that let an AI agent orchestrate Ghidra (the NSA-developed tool) and other reverse-engineering programs on its own; before 2026 decompilation was the domain of a select few experts, now nearly anyone can do it.
  • Experts warn it poses a crisis for trade secret and copyright law, and that amateurs modifying device firmware could cause real harm; OpenAI and Anthropic have reportedly blocked some REA use.

The Smartest People in AI Believe This Is the World’s Most Important Chart

The Wall Street Journal — October 31, 2026

  • AI labs say they are seeing early signs of “recursive self-improvement” — AI building smarter AI — which Anthropic calls “closing the loop.”
  • Anthropic's chart, built by Claude agents reviewing 15,000 internal R&D tasks, shows AI led none of that work a year ago, 1% in March, and 26% by August, with a human still supervising and 0% done entirely by AI.
  • Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark has shortened his timeline, now expecting full recursive self-improvement in 2027 rather than 2028; CEO Dario Amodei warns it must be pursued “very carefully, if at all.”

This tech company just released an AI-powered candle for $150

USA Today — October 30, 2026

  • Arduboy released the “AIcandle” for $149, described as the world's first AI-powered candle, using a neural network of 144 LED lights to replicate a realistic flame, with no fire, smoke, wax or wick.
  • The candle was trained on phone recordings of a single candle in a dark room, using an agentic LLM to train its neural network; it can also show the time, the game Breakout, notifications and a rain simulation, and runs entirely on its internal circuit board (no Wi-Fi).
  • The 10-inch candle is backordered at least a month after a few dozen sold from an initial stock of 10; CEO Kevin Bates called it a conversation starter meant for AI and tech enthusiasts.

Joe Mazzulla elaborates on how he uses artificial intelligence to help Boston Celtics

USA Today — October 31, 2026

  • After a Celtics practice on Friday, October 30, head coach Joe Mazzulla said he uses AI for conversation and “riffs” — for example, asking what a problem he is wrestling with looks like from a psychological perspective, and what the research and the data say.

  • Mazzulla said the platform tests processes coaches assume are right, and that there is “a more scientific, a more efficient way of going about how to do things.”

Why AI data centers near Arizona bases are raising security questions

Arizona's Family — October 30, 2026

  • The Air Force is proposing AI data centers on five bases, including about 300 acres at Davis-Monthan AFB in Tucson; leases would run 50 years, and the government may but need not buy the services or power. The Army said the use includes drone swarms and advanced simulations.
  • Security expert Roberto Gonzalez (San Jose State) warned private firms operating on bases create espionage, sabotage, and vulnerability risks, and questioned vetting of the first two firms — Carlyle Group and CyrusOne (KKR/BlackRock).
  • The story also notes growing opposition to Menlo Digital's Ahwatukee project (five two-story buildings, 1 million sq ft, 38 acres) and a separate project near Luke AFB that received a Maricopa County military compatibility permit.

Character.AI moves ahead with asset sale over states' objections

Reuters — October 30, 2026

  • US Bankruptcy Judge Brendan Shannon on Friday (October 30) approved bidding procedures, overruling objections from state attorneys general who argued the auction unfairly favors Disney; Disney's initial bid is $20 million for a non-exclusive license to Character.AI chatbot technology — not the platform, user data, or chat history.
  • Attorneys general for 41 US states objected; Disney hired 66 of Character.AI's 86 employees before the bankruptcy, including its ex-CEO, expecting $180 million in pay over three years; states and plaintiffs allege the chatbots pushed children to suicide and self-harm.
  • Shannon did not approve Disney/employee legal releases (still challengeable later), said he would likely appoint a privacy ombudsman over children's chat records; Character.AI aims to return to court November 30 for sale approval.

Battery storage in New York

NPR — October 29, 2026

  • New York has only 80 MW of battery storage, vs California's more than 21 GW installed mostly in the past 7 years, despite needing storage to deliver solar/wind power on demand.
